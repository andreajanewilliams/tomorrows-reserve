Method

How a claim is checked.

One day of hourly pumping, one declared tank policy, and a contract that accepts or refuses the carbon claim. Everything below is what a reviewer needs to repeat the check.

The rule

A day’s carbon saving counts only if all of these hold:

  • The tank never drops below the 4,000 m³ minimum or above its 24,000 m³ capacity.
  • No hour pumps more than 6,000 m³.
  • The tank ends the day back at its starting 12,000 m³: no water owed to tomorrow, none parked as surplus.
  • The whole day uses less attributed CO₂ than normal pumping.

These are this experiment’s declared values, fixed in the contract when it is deployed.

The carbon number

Each hour’s pumped water uses energy (0.5 kWh per m³ here), multiplied by that hour’s grid carbon intensity. The plan is compared over all 24 hours with normal pumping, which pumps what the town uses each hour.

Equal volumes use equal energy, so this measures timing: attributed CO₂, not electricity saved or proven avoided emissions.

The data

Measured German hourly water demand, paired by hour with GB grid carbon intensity (NESO Carbon Intensity API, CC BY 4.0), for 25 source-complete days in February 2020.

The tank, pumps and pumping plans are simulated. Sources are pinned by hash in every evidence file.

Who does what

  1. Operator locks the exact hourly plan on the chain (an input digest).
  2. Auditor signs the evidence: demand, pumping, carbon intensity, model and data versions.
  3. Contract re-checks every hour and settles only against the evidence the auditor signed, so the operator cannot issue a receipt alone. A refusal is a failed transaction with a reason, such as WaterDebt, ReserveBreach or NoCarbonBenefit.
  4. Anyone can replay the evidence file on a fresh local chain with npm run verify. Any edit breaks the signatures; a verified refusal stays a refusal.

A reviewer’s checklist

  • The plan’s digest matches the file you were given.
  • The tank policy matches what was agreed.
  • npm run verify reports the evidence as valid.
  • A receipt was issued before relying on the claim.
  • The data sources match the pinned versions.

What this does not show

  • The tank and pumps are simulated; a plan is not proof a pump ran.
  • Both roles run on one machine; the public demo replays recorded runs from a local chain.
  • The plan is committed after the day, not in advance.
  • No field trial has been done.

    The model

    Learn demand. Keep the arithmetic explicit.

    140 gradient-boosted trees predict the next day’s hourly demand using calendar features and earlier consumption. The pump schedule is a constrained linear programme, not an AI-generated instruction.

    More detail

    Missing, non-positive and duplicate-hour days are excluded from the raw source. No future weather and no test observations are used to fit the model. Empirical intervals are not safety guarantees.

    The evidence boundary
    Observed
    German hourly water demand; historical estimated GB grid intensity.
    Learned
    Day-ahead demand forecast and held-out error.
    Assumed
    One tank; ideal hourly flow feedback; 0.5 kWh/m³; constant pump efficiency.
    Demonstrated
    Model accounting, conservative storage constraints and local contract enforcement.
    Not established
    Physical safety, causal grid emissions avoided, field savings or commercial adoption.

    What a blockchain adds here

    A shared contract applies the same rule for every party; it cannot make the input data true.

    More detail

    A utility, flexibility buyer and auditor can share the same day-level accounting rules and see the same settled receipt. Solidity recomputes all 24 water balances and the carbon arithmetic. It cannot authenticate a physical sensor, make a dishonest auditor honest, or prevent a claim in an unrelated deployment. Production use needs metering, identity governance and a shared asset registry.

    Simulated tank and pumps; real water demand and grid carbon data. A plan is not proof a pump ran, and both local roles run on one machine.

    What this does not claim

    • It does not prove a pump actually ran or that a meter is honest. Inputs are attested claims.
    • The tank and pumps are simulated. Water demand is measured German data, paired with a GB grid carbon scenario.
    • Figures are timing-based attributed CO₂ at average grid intensity, not electricity saved or proven avoided emissions.
    • The forecast model’s smaller safety margins do not prove equal safety.
    • In the local demo both roles run on one machine; for a single operator a signed database could do the same job.
    • No carbon credits, tokens or payments are issued.
