    Why the last hour matters

    You can’t borrow tomorrow’s water.

    A cheap-looking schedule can stop early and leave a depleted reservoir. PumpProof requires the same starting and ending volume, so deferred pumping cannot masquerade as a carbon benefit.

    The model

    Learn demand. Keep the arithmetic explicit.

    140 gradient-boosted trees predict the next day’s hourly demand using calendar features and earlier consumption. The pump schedule is a constrained linear programme, not an AI-generated instruction.

    Missing, non-positive and duplicate-hour days are excluded from the raw source. No future weather and no test observations are used to fit the model. Empirical intervals are not safety guarantees.

    The evidence boundary
    Observed
    German hourly water demand; historical estimated GB grid intensity.
    Learned
    Day-ahead demand forecast and held-out error.
    Assumed
    One tank; ideal hourly flow feedback; 0.5 kWh/m³; constant pump efficiency.
    Demonstrated
    Model accounting, conservative storage constraints and local contract enforcement.
    Not established
    Physical safety, causal grid emissions avoided, field savings or commercial adoption.

    What a blockchain adds here

    A utility, flexibility buyer and auditor can share the same day-level accounting rules and see the same settled receipt. Solidity recomputes all 24 water balances and the carbon arithmetic. It cannot authenticate a physical sensor, make a dishonest auditor honest, or prevent a claim in an unrelated deployment. Production use needs metering, identity governance and a shared asset registry.
