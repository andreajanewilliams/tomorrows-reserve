# Native results and limits

The numerical authority is `data/comparison-evaluation.json`, generated by `npm run compare`. Actual external contract outcomes are in `data/external-evm-results.json`, generated by `npm run fixtures:evm`. Regenerating comparison clears its execution annotations; rerun fixture execution afterwards. No Python result is inserted into the native report.

The cohort contains 25 source-complete days, 4–28 February 2020. Dates 1–3 and 29 are excluded for unavailable eligible demand/source coverage; the report lists the precise included/excluded dates. The gross baseline is 124,946.871 kg attributed operational CO₂. Gross reduction is 100 × sum(baseline − operation) / sum(baseline), including failed and negative days. Eligible-only and common-eligible summaries have their own denominators and explicit date lists. Common eligibility means the intersection of model, persistence and honest rule; the depleted rule has no eligible days.

The model reduces gross attributed CO₂ by 9.44042% (24/25 numerically eligible), persistence by 8.67546% (23/25), and the illustrative honest rule by 4.92450% (22/25). The depleted rule shows 19.36190% apparent reduction and is ineligible on all days. All 50 rule fixtures actually executed: 22 accepted, three NoCarbonBenefit refusals, 25 WaterDebt refusals. Optimised-policy comparison rows remain labelled numerical; the legacy contract regression separately executes all 25 original model days.

The model's 24 February failure has 761.294 m³ terminal **surplus**, not debt, and −617.289032 kg raw carbon difference. Persistence on 14 February restores storage but has −0.121 kg raw difference: its only violation is NoCarbonBenefit. The honest rule is refused for no carbon benefit on 14, 22 and 24 February. Every depleted-rule fixture ends at 5,000 m³, 7,000 m³ below its starting storage.

Native reconstructed calibration uses the same 179 July–December 2019 days and the original finite-sample higher-quantile convention, without retraining. Model buffer/headroom are 2,676.280580043891 m³ and 420.1595340472509 m³/hour; persistence uses 4,649 m³ and 683 m³/hour.

Full-year daily cumulative margin coverage is 71.3873% versus 82.0809%; hourly absolute coverage is 86.9340% versus 89.9807%. February daily coverage is 96% versus 100%. These are forecast-error coverage rates, not controller outage or safety rates. Both the buffer and pump headroom change. Forecast-specific operating margins improved simulated carbon performance; equivalent operational safety has not been established.

## Python/native discrepancy

`node scripts/rounding-diagnostic.mjs` isolates the grid-rounding difference. Python ties-to-even instead of native `Math.round` changes 137/600 actual hourly intensities and lowers the cohort baseline to 124,795.357 kg. The rule reductions then become 4.9247553% and 19.3630509%, matching the supplied Python rule reference. The product retains its original native rounding.

Rounding alone does not explain the optimised-policy discrepancy. With ties-to-even grid values but the native LP solver, reductions are 9.4641388% and 8.6827100%, versus supplied Python references approximately 9.38894% and 8.62620%. SciPy HiGHS and javascript-lp-solver differ; the remaining solver/replay numerical difference has not been fully isolated. We publish the native results rather than asserting parity.

Native reconstruction of annual trigonometric features also has threshold sensitivity. An independent in-memory diagnostic found Python/JS sine/cosine differences up to 1.11e−16 changing 58 of 8,304 tree predictions. Original Python-feature MAE remains 221.2618843; reconstructed JS-feature MAE is 221.2332225. The requested margins and coverage agree, and the stored February feature/prediction regression matches. The frozen model and source files were not changed.

## Scope of every claim

Measured German demand is paired by hour index with a GB grid scenario. Tank, pump and energy intensity are hypothetical. Generated policies use idealised current-hour demand feedback; there is no hydraulic validation of this single-tank workflow. Equal-volume traces consume equal energy under 0.5 kWh/m³; the difference is timing-based attributed CO₂, not electricity saved or causal avoided emissions. Peak power means hourly average pumping power, not a measured instantaneous peak or tariff saving.

There is no utility pilot, customer validation, measured field saving or carbon-credit eligibility. Local roles are controlled by one machine. The contract trusts source attestation; duplicate protection is bounded by each experiment contract. Commitments occur after the historical date. Fresh EVM replay does not authenticate the original chain history. The preserved Hydraulic lab is an older separate experiment, not evidence for the new trace receipt.
